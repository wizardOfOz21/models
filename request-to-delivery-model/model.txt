Request-to-delivery model

An instance of the stage metamodel. It describes how a loosely given request becomes a deliverable in a required form, in three stages.

Key concepts

The request arrives raw. Turning it into something you can act on is real work, and it gets its own stage.
The result is delivered to someone, in a form requested in advance. That form is dictated from outside and may be awkward for the work itself, so the working form and the delivered form are allowed to differ.
Internals of the working stage are scaffolding. They exist only there and disappear at delivery.

Framing

Description: turning the request as given into something you can act on.
Inputs: the request, plus any source material.
Outputs: a clear picture of what is needed, and a plan.
Internals: notes, questions, assumptions, a map of the material.

Working

Description: doing the actual work, in whatever form suits the person doing it.
Inputs: the picture and the plan.
Outputs: the result in working form.
Internals: the working representation, such as splitting into parts, drafts, experiments.

Delivering

Description: converting the working form into the required form and handing it over.
Inputs: the result in working form, and the requested form.
Outputs: the deliverable.
Internals: assembly, cleanup, a final check against the requested form.

Links: framing leads to working, which leads to delivering.
